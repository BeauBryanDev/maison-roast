# Classic European Coffee Shop Recipe Book

A collection of classic and comforting recipes designed for a
traditional European-style coffee shop. Each recipe emphasizes familiar
ingredients, careful preparation, and the warm, handcrafted character
associated with classic café baking and cuisine.

## 1. Classic Apple Pie

### Description

Classic Apple Pie is a timeless dessert made with tender apples, warm
spices, and a buttery pastry crust. The combination of crisp pastry and
softly baked apples makes it an ideal companion for coffee or tea. For a
traditional coffee shop presentation, serve it slightly warm with
whipped cream, vanilla ice cream, or simply a light dusting of powdered
sugar.

### Ingredients

**For the pastry:** - 320 g all-purpose flour - 200 g unsalted butter,
cold and cubed - 60 g granulated sugar - 1/2 teaspoon salt - 90--110 ml
ice-cold water

**For the apple filling:** - 6 medium apples, peeled, cored, and thinly
sliced - 100 g granulated sugar - 40 g brown sugar - 1 1/2 teaspoons
ground cinnamon - 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg - 1 tablespoon lemon
juice - 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour - 1 teaspoon vanilla extract -
20 g unsalted butter

**For finishing:** - 1 egg, beaten - 1 tablespoon milk - 1 tablespoon
coarse sugar, optional

### Preparation

Begin by preparing the pastry. Place the flour, sugar, and salt in a
large mixing bowl and stir to combine. Add the cold cubed butter and
work it into the flour using a pastry cutter or your fingertips. Stop
when the mixture resembles coarse crumbs with a few small pieces of
butter still visible. These pieces of butter will help create a flaky
texture during baking.

Gradually add the ice-cold water, mixing gently until the dough begins
to come together. Avoid adding more water than necessary. Transfer the
dough to a lightly floured work surface and bring it together without
kneading excessively. Divide it into two portions, shape each portion
into a disk, wrap them, and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes.

While the dough rests, prepare the filling. Combine the sliced apples
with granulated sugar, brown sugar, cinnamon, nutmeg, lemon juice,
flour, and vanilla. Toss carefully until every apple slice is coated.
Allow the mixture to stand for approximately 10 minutes so the flavors
can develop.

Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F). Roll out one portion of pastry into a
circle large enough to cover the bottom and sides of a 23 cm (9-inch)
pie dish. Carefully transfer the pastry into the dish and gently press
it into place without stretching it.

Pour the apple mixture into the pastry shell and distribute it evenly.
Dot the surface with small pieces of butter. Roll out the second portion
of dough and place it over the apples. Trim the excess pastry and press
the edges together to seal the pie. Create several small slits in the
top crust to allow steam to escape.

Brush the crust with the beaten egg mixed with milk. Sprinkle with
coarse sugar if desired. Place the pie on a baking tray and bake for
approximately 50--60 minutes, or until the crust is deeply golden and
the apple filling is bubbling through the vents.

Allow the pie to cool for at least 30 minutes before slicing. This
resting period allows the filling to thicken and prevents the slices
from becoming excessively runny. Serve warm or at room temperature with
coffee, tea, whipped cream, or vanilla ice cream.


## 2. Classic Tiramisu

### Description

Tiramisu is one of Italy's most recognizable desserts. Layers of
coffee-soaked ladyfingers and a rich mascarpone cream create a delicate
balance of bitterness, sweetness, and creaminess. Its texture improves
considerably after several hours of refrigeration, making it
particularly suitable for preparation in advance.

### Ingredients

-   500 g mascarpone cheese
-   4 large eggs, separated
-   100 g granulated sugar
-   300 g ladyfinger biscuits
-   300 ml strong espresso or very strong coffee, cooled
-   2 tablespoons Marsala wine or coffee liqueur, optional
-   Unsweetened cocoa powder, for dusting
-   Pinch of salt

### Preparation

Prepare the espresso first and allow it to cool completely. If desired,
stir in the Marsala or coffee liqueur. The coffee should be strong but
not hot when the biscuits are assembled, because hot liquid can make
them collapse too quickly.

Separate the eggs carefully. Place the yolks in a large bowl with the
sugar and whisk until the mixture becomes pale, thick, and creamy. Add
the mascarpone gradually, mixing until the cream is smooth and free of
lumps. Do not overmix, as excessive agitation can make the mascarpone
mixture loose.

In a separate clean bowl, whisk the egg whites with a pinch of salt
until they form firm peaks. Fold the whipped whites into the mascarpone
mixture in several additions. Use a gentle folding motion rather than
vigorous stirring. This preserves the air incorporated into the egg
whites and gives the finished cream a lighter texture.

Dip each ladyfinger briefly into the cooled coffee. Do not soak the
biscuits for too long; they should absorb enough coffee to become moist
while remaining firm enough to hold their shape. Arrange a layer of
soaked biscuits in the bottom of a serving dish.

Spread approximately half of the mascarpone cream over the biscuits and
smooth the surface with a spatula. Add another layer of coffee-dipped
ladyfingers, followed by the remaining mascarpone cream. Smooth the top
carefully.

Cover the tiramisu and refrigerate for at least 6 hours, preferably
overnight. During this time, the biscuits absorb the coffee and the
mascarpone cream sets into a cohesive, creamy dessert.

Just before serving, cover the surface generously with unsweetened cocoa
powder. For a traditional coffee shop presentation, cut or spoon the
tiramisu into neat portions and serve chilled alongside a small
espresso.


## 3. French Crème Brûlée

### Description

French Crème Brûlée is a classic baked custard distinguished by its
silky vanilla interior and thin layer of caramelized sugar. The contrast
between the cool, creamy custard and the crisp caramel surface makes it
an elegant yet relatively simple café dessert.

### Ingredients

-   500 ml heavy cream
-   1 vanilla bean, or 2 teaspoons pure vanilla extract
-   6 large egg yolks
-   80 g granulated sugar
-   Pinch of salt
-   4--6 tablespoons fine granulated sugar, for caramelizing

### Preparation

Preheat the oven to 150°C (300°F). Place four to six shallow ramekins in
a deep baking dish. Having the ramekins ready before preparing the
custard will make the final assembly easier.

If using a vanilla bean, split it lengthwise with a sharp knife and
scrape out the seeds. Place the seeds and pod into a saucepan with the
heavy cream. Heat the cream gently over medium-low heat until it is hot
and fragrant but not boiling. Remove the saucepan from the heat and
allow the vanilla to infuse for approximately 10 minutes.

Meanwhile, place the egg yolks, sugar, and salt in a bowl. Whisk gently
until the sugar is incorporated and the yolks become slightly lighter in
color. Avoid creating excessive foam, since a smooth custard is the
goal.

Gradually pour the warm cream into the egg yolks while whisking
continuously. Add the cream slowly so the yolks warm gradually rather
than cooking suddenly. Once combined, strain the custard through a fine
sieve into a clean container. This removes the vanilla pod and any small
pieces of cooked egg, producing a particularly smooth final texture.

Divide the custard evenly among the ramekins. Pour hot water into the
baking dish around the ramekins until it reaches approximately halfway
up their sides. This water bath provides gentle, even heat and helps
prevent the custard from becoming grainy.

Carefully transfer the baking dish to the oven and bake for
approximately 30--40 minutes. The edges should be set while the center
should still have a slight, controlled wobble when the ramekins are
gently moved.

Remove the ramekins from the water bath and allow them to cool at room
temperature. Cover and refrigerate for at least 3 hours, preferably
longer, until thoroughly chilled.

When ready to serve, pat the surface gently if any moisture has
accumulated. Sprinkle a thin, even layer of granulated sugar over each
custard. Use a culinary torch to caramelize the sugar, moving the flame
continuously until the sugar melts and turns deep golden brown. Allow
the caramel to cool for one or two minutes so it becomes crisp.

Serve immediately after caramelizing. The ideal result is a cold, smooth
vanilla custard beneath a thin, glass-like caramel shell.

## 4. Sicilian Cannoli

### Description

Cannoli are traditional Sicilian pastries consisting of crisp fried
pastry shells filled with sweetened ricotta. The shells provide a
delicate crunch while the ricotta filling contributes a creamy, mildly
sweet center. They are particularly attractive in a vintage European
café display.

### Ingredients

**For the shells:** - 250 g all-purpose flour - 30 g granulated sugar -
30 g unsalted butter - 1 tablespoon unsweetened cocoa powder - 1/2
teaspoon ground cinnamon - Pinch of salt - 1 egg - 60 ml Marsala wine -
Vegetable oil, for frying

**For the filling:** - 500 g whole-milk ricotta, thoroughly drained -
100 g powdered sugar - 1 teaspoon vanilla extract - 60 g mini chocolate
chips - 40 g chopped pistachios, optional - Candied orange peel,
optional

### Preparation

Begin by draining the ricotta thoroughly. Place it in a fine sieve or
cheesecloth-lined strainer and refrigerate it for several hours,
preferably overnight. Removing excess moisture is essential because a
wet filling can make the cannoli shells soft.

To make the shells, combine the flour, sugar, cocoa powder, cinnamon,
and salt in a bowl. Add the butter and work it into the dry ingredients
until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs. Add the egg and Marsala
gradually, mixing until a firm dough forms.

Transfer the dough to a lightly floured surface and knead it for several
minutes until smooth and elastic. Wrap it and allow it to rest for
approximately 30--60 minutes. Resting makes the dough easier to roll and
shape.

Roll the dough very thinly, approximately 1--2 mm thick. Cut circles
approximately 10 cm (4 inches) in diameter. Wrap each circle around a
metal cannoli tube, overlapping the edges slightly. Seal the overlap
with a small amount of beaten egg, taking care not to glue the pastry to
the metal tube.

Heat the frying oil to approximately 175--180°C (350--355°F). Fry the
shells in small batches until they become crisp, blistered, and deep
golden brown. This generally takes only a few minutes. Remove them
carefully and drain on paper towels.

Once the shells are cool enough to handle, gently slide them off the
metal tubes. Allow them to cool completely before filling.

For the filling, combine the drained ricotta, powdered sugar, and
vanilla until smooth. Fold in the chocolate chips and, if desired,
chopped pistachios or finely diced candied orange peel. Refrigerate the
filling until needed.

Fill the cannoli shortly before serving. Use a piping bag to pipe the
ricotta cream into both ends of each shell, ensuring the filling reaches
the center. Do not fill them too far in advance, as the moisture from
the ricotta will gradually soften the crisp pastry.

Finish with powdered sugar, chopped pistachios, chocolate pieces, or
candied orange peel. Serve fresh with espresso or a strong Italian-style
coffee.


## 5. Classic Carrot Cake

### Description

Carrot Cake is a moist, warmly spiced cake made with grated carrots and
traditionally finished with a smooth cream cheese frosting. Its rustic
appearance and rich flavor make it well suited to a classic coffee shop
menu.

### Ingredients

**For the cake:** - 300 g all-purpose flour - 2 teaspoons baking
powder - 1 teaspoon baking soda - 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon - 1/2
teaspoon ground nutmeg - 1/2 teaspoon salt - 200 ml vegetable oil - 180
g brown sugar - 80 g granulated sugar - 4 large eggs - 1 teaspoon
vanilla extract - 300 g finely grated carrots - 100 g chopped walnuts or
pecans - 80 g raisins, optional

**For the frosting:** - 250 g cream cheese, softened - 80 g unsalted
butter, softened - 150 g powdered sugar - 1 teaspoon vanilla extract - 1
teaspoon lemon juice

### Preparation

Preheat the oven to 175°C (350°F). Grease and line two 20 cm (8-inch)
round cake pans, or prepare one larger rectangular baking pan if the
cake will be served in café-style slices.

In a bowl, combine the flour, baking powder, baking soda, cinnamon,
nutmeg, and salt. Stir thoroughly so the leavening agents and spices are
evenly distributed throughout the flour.

In a separate large bowl, whisk the oil, brown sugar, granulated sugar,
eggs, and vanilla until smooth. The mixture does not need to be whipped
extensively. The oil contributes to the characteristic moist texture of
carrot cake.

Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture and stir just until
no large pockets of flour remain. Avoid overmixing, since excessive
mixing can make the cake dense.

Fold in the grated carrots, chopped nuts, and raisins if using. Make
sure the carrots are distributed evenly throughout the batter. The
mixture will be relatively thick, which is normal.

Divide the batter between the prepared pans and smooth the tops. Bake
for approximately 25--35 minutes, depending on the pan size. Test the
center with a wooden skewer; it should come out clean or with only a few
moist crumbs attached.

Allow the cakes to cool in their pans for approximately 10 minutes. Then
transfer them to a wire rack and allow them to cool completely. Do not
frost the cake while it is warm, because the frosting may melt and
become unstable.

To prepare the frosting, beat the softened cream cheese and butter until
smooth. Add the powdered sugar gradually, followed by the vanilla and
lemon juice. Beat only until creamy and homogeneous.

Place one cake layer on a serving plate and spread a generous layer of
frosting across the top. Place the second layer over it and cover the
top and sides with the remaining frosting. For a rustic coffee shop
presentation, the frosting does not need to be perfectly smooth.

Refrigerate the finished cake briefly before slicing. Serve in generous
café-style portions with coffee, cappuccino, or black tea.


## 6. Croque-Madame

### Description

Croque-Madame is a classic French café sandwich made with toasted bread,
ham, cheese, béchamel sauce, and a fried egg. It is the richer, more
elaborate counterpart to the Croque-Monsieur and works particularly well
as a savory brunch or light lunch offering.

### Ingredients

**For the sandwich:** - 8 slices brioche or good-quality white bread - 8
slices cooked ham - 160 g Gruyère or Comté cheese, grated - 30 g
unsalted butter

**For the béchamel:** - 30 g unsalted butter - 30 g all-purpose flour -
300 ml whole milk - Pinch of nutmeg - Salt and black pepper

**For the topping:** - 4 large eggs - 1 tablespoon vegetable oil or
butter - Additional grated Gruyère, optional

### Preparation

Begin with the béchamel sauce. Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium
heat. Add the flour and whisk continuously for approximately one minute
to form a smooth roux. Do not allow it to become dark; the sauce should
remain pale.

Gradually add the milk while whisking continuously. Continue cooking
until the mixture becomes smooth and thick enough to coat the back of a
spoon. Season with salt, black pepper, and a small pinch of nutmeg.
Remove from the heat and keep warm.

Preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F), or prepare a grill/broiler for
finishing the sandwiches. Spread a thin layer of béchamel over four
slices of bread. Add a slice or two of ham and a generous amount of
grated Gruyère or Comté.

Cover each sandwich with another slice of bread. Lightly butter the
outside of the top slices. Place the sandwiches on a baking tray and
spread another layer of béchamel over the top of each one. Finish with
additional grated cheese.

Bake or broil until the cheese is completely melted and the tops are
golden and bubbling. Watch carefully during the final minutes because
the cheese can brown quickly.

While the sandwiches are finishing, heat a frying pan with a small
amount of butter or oil. Crack the eggs into the pan and fry them until
the whites are fully set while the yolks remain soft and glossy. The
yolk should ideally remain slightly runny so that it becomes part of the
sauce when the sandwich is cut.

Remove the toasted sandwiches from the oven and transfer them to serving
plates. Place one fried egg on top of each sandwich. Season lightly with
freshly ground black pepper.

Serve immediately while the bread is crisp, the cheese is molten, the
béchamel is warm, and the egg yolk remains soft. A simple green salad
makes an excellent accompaniment.

## 7. Red Fruits Cake

### Description

Red Fruits Cake is an elegant café-style dessert built around a light
sponge cake, delicate cream, and a generous selection of fresh red
berries. Strawberries, raspberries, and red currants provide natural
sweetness and acidity, creating a refreshing contrast to the soft cake
and cream.

### Ingredients

**For the sponge:** - 180 g all-purpose flour - 1 1/2 teaspoons baking
powder - Pinch of salt - 120 g unsalted butter, softened - 150 g
granulated sugar - 3 large eggs - 1 teaspoon vanilla extract - 80 ml
whole milk

**For the cream:** - 300 ml heavy cream, chilled - 40 g powdered sugar -
1 teaspoon vanilla extract

**For the fruit topping:** - 200 g strawberries - 150 g raspberries -
100 g red currants - 100 g blackberries - 1--2 tablespoons granulated
sugar, optional - Fresh mint leaves, optional

### Preparation

Preheat the oven to 175°C (350°F). Grease and line a 20 cm (8-inch)
round cake pan with parchment paper.

Combine the flour, baking powder, and salt in a bowl. In a separate
large bowl, beat the softened butter and sugar until pale and creamy.
This step incorporates air into the mixture and helps produce a lighter
sponge.

Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition. Stir in
the vanilla extract. Gradually add the dry ingredients, alternating with
the milk. Begin and finish with the dry ingredients, mixing only until
the batter becomes smooth.

Transfer the batter to the prepared pan and smooth the surface. Bake for
approximately 25--30 minutes, or until the top is golden and a wooden
skewer inserted into the center comes out clean.

Allow the cake to cool in the pan for approximately 10 minutes before
transferring it to a wire rack. Let it cool completely before adding the
cream and fruit.

While the cake cools, prepare the cream. Pour the chilled heavy cream
into a cold mixing bowl. Add the powdered sugar and vanilla and whip
until soft-to-medium peaks form. The cream should hold its shape but
remain smooth and spreadable.

Wash the berries carefully and dry them thoroughly. Slice the
strawberries into attractive pieces while keeping some whole for
decoration. If the berries are particularly tart, toss them gently with
a small amount of sugar and allow them to stand for 5--10 minutes.

Once the sponge is completely cool, place it on a serving plate. Spread
the whipped cream evenly across the top, leaving a small border around
the edge. Arrange the strawberries, raspberries, red currants, and
blackberries over the cream.

For a more refined café presentation, arrange the fruit in overlapping
groups rather than scattering it randomly. Keep the natural colors and
shapes visible. Finish with a few small mint leaves if desired.

Refrigerate the cake for approximately 30 minutes before serving. This
allows the cream to stabilize and makes slicing easier. Serve chilled
with espresso, cappuccino, or tea.
